# Substrate · Volume III · Aesthetic Continuity

Volume III inherits the SUBSTRATE design philosophy from Volumes I and II without amendment. The same ink-on-cream palette (paper-warm ground, deep ink, single oxidized vermilion accent with verdigris and ochre annotations); the same hairline rules, strict horizontal registers, and condensed display caps for stratum names; the same monospace marginalia and italic serif scale descriptors; the same depth-scale tick rules running the full height of every page like a geologist's column. The reader who opens any plate from any volume should not be able to tell which volume produced it without reading the page badge — "Volume I," "Volume II," "Volume III." The visual language is the inheritance; the content is what advances.

The single change at Volume III is the badge. Plates in this volume read "VOL III" or "PLATE [Roman numeral] OF VI" / "OF XI" — to distinguish the agent-layer atlas from the parent full-stack atlas while preserving the convention that this is one continuing artifact rather than a separate work. The strata themselves use Roman numerals descending from X (most visible: end-user surfaces) to I (most fundamental: foundation models with agentic capability) plus four wrapping meta-strata labeled A–D, mirroring the parent's XIV-down convention at agent-layer resolution. The Master Plate uses the letter "M" in its badge for the same reason Volume I's Master Plate used "M" (the Big Shoulders display face lacks a clean omega glyph).

The work must continue to read as if rescued from the print drawer of a vanished discipline of natural philosophers who spent three generations developing a visual language for documenting the architecture of intelligence itself. The seriousness is the point.
