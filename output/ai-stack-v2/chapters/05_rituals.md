# Chapter 5 — Operating Rituals

The inline Apply drills at the end of Ch 1–4 are how you stay sharp on one chapter at a time. This chapter is how those drills compound into a practice instead of staying one-off exercises. Four cadences carry the work: weekly (~10 min), monthly (~15 min), quarterly (~60 min), twice-yearly (~half-day). If you only do one of these, do the monthly conviction update — it is the operational core. The rest accumulates around it.

Rituals beat resolutions because they are time-boxed and place-anchored. A resolution is "I'll keep the tracker current." A ritual is "First Sunday, 9 PM, 15 minutes, this checklist, these five sections." The first is aspiration; the second runs. Each ritual below specifies four things: when it fires, what you do, what artifact it produces, and — equally important — what you explicitly do not do. The "do not" half is where most operating systems break, because the impulse to "refresh everything while you're in there" is what makes the cadence slip from 15 minutes to 90 and then to never.

This is the connective tissue between Ch 3 (the bets) and Ch 4 (the tracker). It is also the discipline that keeps Ch 2's frameworks from drifting into validation theater (see §3.3 score-discipline note).

## §5.1 Weekly reflection (~10 min)

### When
A fixed time each week. Sunday evening is the default — calendar-block it as a recurring invite. The point is the cadence, not the day.

### What you do
Three prompts, roughly 3 minutes each:

1. **What did you hear this week that re-priced any bet?** Write the bet number (Ch 3 §3.1) and the signal in one sentence. If nothing — write "no signal this week" rather than skipping the prompt. The act of forcing the noticing matters more than the entry; an empty week, named, is a real datapoint.

2. **What crux moved?** Refer to the 5 cruxes (Ch 3 §3.3). If any of them saw a public datapoint this week — an earnings print, a regulatory filing, a benchmark, a leak — log the crux number and the datapoint in one sentence.

3. **What conversation could you have had that you didn't?** Name the person, the topic, why you didn't reach out. This prompt surfaces lazy social action — the dominant failure mode for an operator running a search.

### Where it lands
A weekly note in your normal notes system (Notion, Obsidian, Apple Notes — doesn't matter). The format is irrelevant; the cadence is what compounds. Don't migrate weekly notes into Ch 4 unless an item escalates into a tracker-worthy event.

### What you don't do
Don't update the tracker tables. Don't re-rate convictions. Don't re-read framework chapters. The weekly is a noticing ritual, not a maintenance ritual. If an item demands a tracker update, hold it for the monthly — or, if it's genuinely urgent (a crux resolved, a risk fired), use the trigger-based path in §10 of the spec.

## §5.2 Monthly conviction ritual (~15 min)

### When
First Sunday of each month. Calendar-block it as a recurring invite, ideally adjacent to the weekly slot so the muscle memory is unified.

### What you do
Five steps in strict order — do not skip:

1. **Re-rate each bet** (Ch 3 §3.1). For each of the 7, mark conviction ★★★ through ★★★★★ — same as last month, up, or down. If down, write the one-sentence reason. If up, the bar is higher: write what evidence shifted and which leading indicator carried it.

2. **Mark which leading indicators moved.** For each bet, scan its "Leading indicators to watch" list (Ch 3 §3.1) and tag movers. The point isn't to update every cell; it's to see whether the indicators you wrote down are the ones actually doing work. If three months pass and no indicator on a given bet has moved, the indicator list is stale, not the bet.

3. **Tag any crux that fired** (Ch 3 §3.3). If a crux saw a definitive answer-event during the month, mark it resolved and re-rank affected bets per the consequence written in Ch 3. A crux is not "moved a little"; it is answered or unanswered.

4. **Update the bets status board** (Ch 4 §4.2). The conviction column reflects step 1; the "last conviction change" column reflects the date of step 1.

5. **Add one new entry to Ch 4 §4.3 (talent) or §4.4 (capital)** from the last 14 days that you haven't logged yet. This is the catch-up provision for the bi-weekly cadence specified in §10 of the spec — if you've been keeping bi-weekly current, this step is a freebie; if you haven't, it's the safety net.

### Where it lands
Ch 4 §4.2 (bets status board), §4.8 (cruxes status), §4.10 (update log: add a row dated today with one-line summary of what changed).

### What you don't do
Don't redo the Ch 3 bet narratives unless conviction changed by ≥ 1 star. The narratives are stable; the conviction is the live signal. Don't re-score OCQ — that's quarterly (§5.3). Don't archive anything — that's twice-yearly (§5.4). The monthly is the most-likely-to-bloat ritual; protect the 15-minute envelope.

## §5.3 Quarterly deep review (~60 min)

### When
First Sunday of Jan, Apr, Jul, Oct. Calendar-block 90 minutes (the extra 30 absorbs the "while I'm in there" tax).

### What you do
Six tasks in order:

1. **Re-read Ch 2 frameworks** (45 min). Look for: lens definitions you've drifted from; OCQ scores you've inflated (the score-discipline check the spec calls out in §3.3); Wardley placements that no longer hold given the quarter's punctuated equilibria; 7 Powers attributions that have eroded. Mark margin notes; defer edits to the twice-yearly refresh unless something is materially wrong.

2. **Re-check Ch 3 §3.2 risks.** For each of the 5 Structural Risks, the spec mandates quarterly re-check. Mark "still open" or describe what changed in one sentence. A risk that has not been re-examined for 90 days is functionally a risk you have stopped tracking.

3. **Rotate the Ch 1–4 inline Apply drills.** Each drill says "rotate strata each pass" or equivalent — actually do it. Pick three different strata for Ch 1, a different bet for Ch 3, different tracker rows for Ch 4. Rotation is the only thing that keeps the drills from becoming muscle memory on the same three easy items.

4. **Audit the Ch 4 tables.** Drop any "Date" rows older than 12 months that aren't load-bearing. Compress the historical talent moves table to keep the rolling-12-month discipline. This is bookkeeping, not analysis — keep it fast.

5. **Decide on twice-yearly refresh.** If it's January or July, kick off the next ritual (§5.4) for the following Sunday and calendar-block 4 hours. Otherwise skip this step.

6. **Write a 5-sentence end-of-quarter note**: what shifted, what didn't, what you got wrong, what you got right, what conversation you want to have next quarter that you didn't have last quarter. Five sentences — not five paragraphs.

### Where it lands
Ch 4 §4.10 update log entry, with one-line summary. The 5-sentence note can live anywhere; an annual collection of these (four notes a year) becomes its own artifact and is worth reading end-to-end at the twice-yearly refresh.

## §5.4 Twice-yearly major refresh (~half-day)

### When
First Sunday of January and July. Calendar-block 4 hours — this is the only ritual that requires a real time commitment.

### What you do
Six tasks of larger, less-frequent maintenance:

1. **Re-extract from any new source material.** If new reports, books, primary filings, or industry analyses have landed since the last refresh, decide what gets pulled into Ch 1 (strata content) or Ch 2 (framework updates). Source material that hasn't earned a citation in 6 months gets archived, not deleted.

2. **Regenerate plates.** Run `python plates/build_plates.py`. If data has shifted enough that a plate now misleads, edit the plate script and regenerate. Plates that no longer earn their place (per §6 of the spec) get cut, not patched.

3. **Version-bump `_spec.md`.** Increment the version, add a change-log row (§12 of the spec). If any canonical taxonomy item shifted — a new stratum named, a framework retired, a Crux resolved into the history — this is the moment.

4. **Decide on archival.** Move anything genuinely stale into `_archive/`. Don't delete; archive. The point is that a future read should be able to reconstruct why a previous version of the thesis looked the way it did.

5. **Rebuild EPUB.** Run `bash build_epub.sh`. Read at least one chapter end-to-end on a device that is not your computer — phone, tablet, e-reader. Note what reads poorly on the smaller screen and fix it in the next monthly.

6. **Write a 1-page state-of-the-thesis note** for the next 6 months. This is the open-question version of the conviction ritual at quarterly scale: what is the thesis betting on that it wasn't six months ago, and what is it no longer betting on.

### Where it lands
Spec change-log (§12). Ch 4 §4.10. The 1-page note belongs in `_archive/state_of_thesis_YYYY-MM.md` — naming it by date makes the sequence readable in 18 months.

## Apply

Calendar-block all four rituals right now — weekly, monthly, quarterly, twice-yearly. Make them recurring invites with real times of day, not floating intentions. If they aren't on the calendar by the time you close this document, the workbook will drift back into reference clutter inside a quarter. The cost is roughly 90 minutes per quarter once you're in the rhythm. The value is keeping your bets in front of you instead of behind you — and surfacing the one conversation, each week, that you didn't have but should have.
