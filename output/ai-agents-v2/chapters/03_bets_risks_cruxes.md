### Sub-Crux — OSWorld 65% on a Frontier System (Q3 2026)

**The question.** Does any frontier system (Claude, GPT, Gemini) cross 65% reliability on the OSWorld computer-use benchmark by Q3 2026? 65% is the threshold the field treats as the production-deployment marker for narrow back-office computer-use agents.

**Why it matters.** Feeds Crux 5 (inference compute) and Crux 4 (memory) directly. 65% reliability is the binding constraint between supervised computer-use (where it is today, mid-2026) and deployable computer-use for narrow back-office (Job 1, knowledge-worker delegation). ## §3.5 The Agent-Specific Procurement Rubric (Appendix to Bet #1)

This rubric is the **new Volume III artifact** — the agent-specific section of the future Enterprise AI Procurement Operating Standard. It exists because the parent rubric (general AI vendor procurement, V1 framing) covers six counterparty checklists in non-agent-specific form; a generic AI vendor passes the parent rubric and still fails the agent-specific gauntlet on the seven overlays below. The rubric is the demo the Bet #1 Playbook ships with. The F1000 AI Governance lead is the primary reader. The structure follows the Job 6 phase map (Ch 2 §2.4). Each overlay names what the buyer asks, what good evidence looks like, what vapor sounds like, and how to verify.

**1. Tool-boundary policy (Prepare phase).** Ask the vendor for a written tool-by-tool authority list: which tools the agent can invoke unsupervised, which require approval, which are blocked when context is tainted by external content. Good answer: a five-layer defense stack — treat tool returns as untrusted (tag bytes, degrade high-privilege when planning context is tainted); action-confirmation gates for write/pay/send/escalate or reversible-sandbox execution; output validation and schema enforcement; sub-agent privilege separation; continuous red-team. Vapor: "the agent uses your existing approval flows" without a written map. A generic AI vendor does not have this because LLM vendors sell a model, not an agent operating envelope.

**2. Indirect prompt-injection adaptive red-team (Prepare phase).** Ask for the most recent adaptive-adversary test results — not public-corpus benchmark scores. Good evidence: adaptive-adversary numbers between 60–80%, honestly disclosed, with methodology (Promptfoo, Garak, Lakera Red, or equivalent harness) and continuous-refresh cadence. Vapor: 99% PI detection without adaptive-adversary qualification. Verify by asking for the report and the methodology. The generic vendor does not have this because public-corpus benchmarks are what fits the marketing motion; adaptive-adversary numbers are uncomfortable and require ongoing investment.

**3. Action-rollback documentation (Execute phase).** Ask for the action-rollback story for every write tool: if the agent writes the wrong value into the system of record, what is the recovery path? Good answer: write tools wrapped in a reversible-action layer (database row versioning, idempotency keys, dead-letter queues for irreversible side effects with human approval before execution), audit log accessible to the buyer in real time, alerting on anomalous write rates. Vapor: "we have logging." Verify by asking for a sample audit log and the recovery procedure for a synthetic incident. The generic vendor does not have this because LLMs do not write to systems of record; agents do.

**4. Sub-agent privilege separation (Define + Prepare phases).** Ask: when the agent spawns sub-agents, do downstream sub-agents run with reduced permissions? Show the privilege map. Good answer: a documented privilege-separation architecture where planner sub-agents read but cannot write, executor sub-agents write only to whitelisted systems, verifier sub-agents read both but write to neither, with the privilege boundary enforced at the runtime layer (not at the prompt layer). Vapor: "we use the same credentials throughout." This overlay does not exist for non-agent AI vendors because non-agent AI does not spawn sub-agents.

**5. Signed reproducible eval reports (Confirm phase).** Ask for a signed reproducible eval report against the buyer's use case, with model pin, dataset hash, and harness version. Good answer: a written signed report tied to a specific model pin (e.g., claude-opus-4-5-20251101), dataset hash, harness version, regression set of 200–1,000 trajectories curated from production traffic with monthly refresh cadence. Inspect AI compatibility is a plus; OpenTelemetry GenAI conventions for trace ingest are table stakes. Vapor: "our internal benchmark." Verify by reproducing one score on the buyer side; treat unreproducible scores as untrusted. The generic vendor does not have this because eval-as-CI for agent trajectories is a 2026 emerging practice.

**6. EU AI Act Article 14 human-oversight tie-out (Conclude phase).** Ask for the Article 14 conformity-assessment tie-out mapping the vendor's controls to each Article 14 oversight requirement, with named control IDs and reproducible evidence per control. Good answer: a written tie-out, plus annual refresh process documented, plus counterparty-specific evidence packs for each of the buyer's six approval lanes. Vapor: "we have SOC 2." Verify by walking the tie-out line-by-line. The April 2026 Article 14 oversight draft is the active reference; this overlay does not exist for generic AI vendors because Article 14 is agent-and-high-risk-AI-specific.

**7. Sectoral overlays (HIPAA / GLBA / SOC 2 / GDPR right-to-be-forgotten / PCI).** Ask for the sector-specific overlays the buyer's industry requires: HIPAA BAA for healthcare; GLBA controls for financial services; SOC 2 Type II baseline; GDPR Article 17 forget API with auditable deletion confirmation; PCI scope reduction for payment workflows. Good answer: each overlay documented with named control IDs, scope statements, and evidence freshness through annual review. Vapor: "we are SOC 2 compliant" without sector-specific overlays. Generic AI vendors stop at SOC 2; agents handling regulated data require all of these.

**Scoring.** Rate each of the seven overlays on a five-point scale (5 = excellent / verified / signed evidence; 1 = vapor / unverifiable / unwilling to disclose). A vendor scoring below 3 average should not pass an F1000 AI Governance review on agent-specific procurement as of May 2026. The buyer should expect approximately 20% of agent vendors clear an average of 3.5 or higher today; the remaining 80% need a roadmap commitment in writing or a contractual SLA backed by exit terms. The Playbook will maintain a vendor-readiness leaderboard updated quarterly.

This rubric is the live appendix to Bet #1. Update it whenever an EU AI Act guidance lands, an agent-specific incident reprices the field, or a procurement gauntlet surfaces a missing overlay.

## §3.6 Bet Coupling and Risk Propagation

Three coupling patterns matter at agent-layer resolution.

**The three-product, one-buyer compound.** Bet #1 (Procurement Playbook) + Bet #4 (FinOps for Trajectories, folded in as module) + Bet #5 (Enterprise Architecture Audit including memory) sell to the same F1000 buyer. The §3.5 rubric is the demo for Bet #1; the FinOps audit and architecture audit are the follow-on engagements. **Shared-fate clusters when risk fires.** Risk 3 (Anthropic ARR lower bound) repricing Bet #2 also compresses Bet #1's TAM (fewer agent vendors with budget for procurement-readiness advisory) and Bet #4's TAM (lower aggregate token spend across the comp set). The Bet #1 procurement rubric is the most-robust artifact in the portfolio across all five cruxes — by design.
