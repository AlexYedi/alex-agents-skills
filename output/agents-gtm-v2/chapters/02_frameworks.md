Volume I applied five lenses to eighteen full-stack strata; Volume III re-ran the same five lenses at fourteen agent sub-strata. Volume IV runs them again at a much finer grain — the 156-cell intersection of twelve GTM functions with thirteen agent capabilities. The convergence principle is the same as in (V1 Ch 2) and (V3 Ch 2): each lens is mis-calibrated or fashion-driven on its own, but when several independent lenses point at the same cell, that cell is what survives doubt. The job of this chapter is to surface those agreements.

V4 produces three TRIPLE-CONFIRMATION findings that no single lens generated alone. First, the (9, G) procurement gauntlet is the single most asymmetric cell on the Wardley map (F1), it carries the highest Power footing of any bet (F2), and it is the only cell that satisfies two of the five GTM-org JTBD jobs simultaneously (F3). Second, Hebbia ranks #1 for Bet #2 across three independently-derived rankings: F4's NYC composite (under-funded ratio plus equity asymmetry plus pre-Series-C window), F2's JOIN-quality ordering (power-rent quality against Alex's career asymmetry), and F3's career-JTBD test (equity-asymmetry-with-operator-credibility on a 5-7 year horizon). A note on scoring: V4 uses an OCQ /20 scale rather than V1/V3's /15. The fourth dimension is Density — how dense the vendor landscape is at this cell, which is only legible at cell resolution. The methodology note for the Density dimension lives in (Ch 6 §6.3.1). Where this chapter cites OCQ scores they are V4 /20 totals; V3 stratum scores are not directly comparable.

## §2.1 OCQ × Cell Matrix

### Lens definitions (V4 /20 scale)

Carried from V1/V3 with the Density dimension added per (Ch 0) and the spec (§3.4). Each lens scores 1–5 on four dimensions, total /20.

**OPPORTUNITY.** Confidence (evidence weight that the cell is real and procurable today) × Time-to-Monetize (≤90 days = 5; ≥18 months = 1) × Claimability (Alex's specific profile wins versus the next-best operator) × Density (count of OCQ-13+ adjacent cells the position naturally compounds into). The Density dimension makes Bet #1's (9, G) anchor read at 19/20 rather than the 14/15 it would have scored under the V3 rubric — Density-5 reflects the eight high-OCQ cells it spillovers into.

**CHALLENGE.** Severity (how much the constraint reprices) × Probability (likelihood it bites in the planning horizon) × Alex-Exposure (how much of his bet surface is at risk) × Time-Sensitivity (closing the window in months, not years).

**OPEN QUESTION.** Decidability (5 = decidable within 6 months) × Asymmetry (how much bets diverge based on the answer) × Bet-Size (how much the playbook would shift) × Cell-Coverage (how many cells in the matrix re-rate when the answer arrives). Cell-Coverage is the V4 analogue of V1's "asymmetry across the whole tracker." The K-column open questions (OSWorld 65%) score highest on Cell-Coverage because a single resolution event reprices seven cells simultaneously.

The scoring discipline carried from V1 holds: resist 20/20 inflation, let the distribution do the work, and treat the top-15 cells from (Ch 1 §1.4) as the priority queue rather than a validation list. Ties at the cell are broken by Claimability — that is what makes the lens Alex-specific rather than market-neutral.

## §2.4 Ecosystem JTBD (5 GTM-org jobs)

### Why GTM-org jobs not agent-workflow jobs

V3 mapped seven agent-workflow JTBD jobs at the agent-stack level — back-office task, customer-facing conversation, code change, SaaS operation, stay current, procurement, and one new. Those describe what the agent *does*. V4 maps five GTM-org jobs at the federation level — what the GTM org *buys* the agent for. Conflating the two scales is the most common error in vendor narratives.
